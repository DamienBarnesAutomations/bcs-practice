stem: A warehouse keeps reporting stock discrepancies, and the team asks for a better exception report. The analyst instead traces why stock movements are recorded late at every shift handover. Which principle is being applied?
+ Root causes, not symptoms | The report request is treated as a symptom, and the analyst investigates the underlying cause of the discrepancies.
- Business improvement, not IT system change | This principle asks whether the proposed change improves the business at all, but it does not describe tracing a cause.
- Options, not solutions | This principle is about evaluating alternative ways of meeting a need, not about diagnosing why a problem occurs.
- Feasible, contributing requirements | This principle filters requests against constraints and objectives, which is a later activity.
- The entire business change lifecycle | This principle concerns the span of BA involvement, not the depth of investigation.
- Negotiation, not avoidance | This principle concerns resolving conflicting stakeholder views.
why: The request for a report addresses the visible symptom. Checking how and when stock movements are captured looks for the root cause, which is what the first principle asks for.

stem: The POPIT model is used to structure the analysis of a business system. Which four areas does it cover?
+ People; organisation; processes; information and technology | These are the four views shown in the POPIT triangle, with information and technology treated as one band.
- People; processes; technology; strategy | Strategy is not a POPIT view; it sets the context in which POPIT analysis happens.
- Organisation; culture; processes; data | Culture and data are aspects considered within POPIT, but they are not two of its four named views.
- People; information; customers; suppliers | Customers and suppliers are stakeholder categories, not POPIT views.

stem: A business analyst is asked to establish whether a proposed self-service portal is viable and to pull together the costs, benefits and risks so that the board can decide whether to fund it. Which BASF service is this?
+ Feasibility assessment and business case development | The service tests viability and assembles the case on which a funding decision is taken.
- Situation investigation and problem analysis | This service investigates the business situation and defines the problem before options are assessed.
- Business process improvement | This service redesigns or improves processes rather than appraising an investment.
- Requirements definition | This service elicits and specifies what the solution must do, after the decision to proceed.

- Business change deployment | This service supports the rollout and take-up of the solution.
