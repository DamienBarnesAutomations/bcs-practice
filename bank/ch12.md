stem: What does signing off or baselining requirements mean in a linear project?
+ The requirements are confirmed as an accurate statement, become suitable for design and development, and any later change goes through change control | That is what baselining establishes.
- The requirements can no longer be changed for any reason | Baselining puts changes under control rather than forbidding them.
- The requirements have been fully detailed enough to build without further analysis | Baselining confirms accuracy of statement, not complete design detail.
- The project sponsor takes over ownership of every requirement | Owners remain with the business.
- The document is published to the whole organisation | Publication is not the effect described.
- The requirements are frozen until the next project phase meeting | Change control governs changes at any time.
why: The baseline is the moment a document becomes the reference point. Before it, the requirements are drafts; after it, a change is a decision with a cost rather than an edit.

+ Backwards from traceability, which traces a feature back to its origin | Backwards from answers what was the source for this requirement and who raised it.

stem: What does vertical traceability show?
+ How a requirement aligns up or down the hierarchy, ultimately to business values, policies, strategy and objectives | That is the vertical axis the chapter describes.
- How a requirement was delivered | That is horizontal traceability.
- Which version of a requirement is current | That is configuration management.
- Which stakeholder approved the requirement | That is ownership.
- How many requirements belong to each business area | Grouping is not traceability.
- Which tests cover a requirement | Test coverage is a form of horizontal tracing.
why: Vertical traceability is the alignment check: it is how an analyst demonstrates that a solution requirement earns its place by supporting a business requirement, which in turn supports strategy.

stem: What is the difference between change control and configuration management?
+ Change control manages changes that have been requested, while configuration management manages the items that make up the deliverables | The chapter states this distinction explicitly because the two are often confused.
- Change control manages the versions of items, while configuration management manages requests | The definitions are the other way round here.
- They are two names for the same process | The chapter says confusion arises precisely because they are different.
- Change control applies to Agile and configuration management to linear projects | Both apply across approaches.
- Configuration management is the approval step within change control | Approval belongs to change control.
- Change control is done by the project office and configuration management by the analyst | Ownership of the activities is not the distinction.
why: One manages demand for change, the other manages the contents of baselined items. They connect because assessing a change means asking which configuration items it affects.

+ Document the proposed change, analyse it, consult the stakeholders, then decide on the change | Those are the four stages in order.

stem: During development, a team works out further detail of how a baselined requirement will be met. Does that require a formal change to the requirement?
+ No, because emergent detail about how a requirement is fulfilled is not a change to the requirement itself | The chapter distinguishes elaboration from change.
- Yes, because any change to the documentation requires reapproval | Elaboration of how the work is done is not a change to the definition.
- Yes, because the baseline means the requirement is fixed | A baseline controls change rather than preventing elaboration.
- No, because configuration management does not apply in Agile | The distinction applies in both approaches.
- No, because the requirement owner can approve details informally | Approval authority is not the reason.

stem: Why is cross-referencing requirements and documents an element of requirements management?
+ It guards against inconsistency and fragmentation and supports impact analysis when a change is proposed | The chapter gives both reasons.
- It replaces the need for unique identifiers | Identifiers are a separate element.
- It reduces the number of requirements in the catalogue | Cross-referencing does not remove requirements.
