+ Linear early stages to establish feasibility, analysis and design, then multiple increments each comprising development, testing and implementation | That is the incremental structure the chapter illustrates.

stem: What is the fundamental difference between the incremental and the iterative lifecycle?
+ Incremental defines a complete requirements set and overall design up front, while iterative evolves the requirements through iterations | The chapter states this as the key distinction.
- Incremental deploys once, while iterative deploys in increments | Incremental is the one that deploys in increments.

stem: Which of these does the chapter give as a disadvantage of the linear lifecycles?
+ A completely defined set of requirements is likely to be out of date quickly, because the pace of business change is rapid | That is one of the four disadvantages listed.
- The requirements are usually too vague to develop from | The opposite is the risk: over-specification.
