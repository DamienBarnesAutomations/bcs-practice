=== id: ch9-9.6-01
chapter: 9
topic: 9
lo: 9.6
stem: What is the main weakness of a payback calculation as an investment appraisal technique?
+ It ignores the time value of money, so money received in later years is treated as worth the same as money today | That is the chapter's stated limitation, and the reason DCF exists.
- It requires a discount rate that most organisations cannot agree | The discount rate belongs to DCF, not payback.
- It cannot be presented as a cash-flow forecast | A payback calculation is precisely a cash-flow forecast.
- It assumes benefits are spread evenly across the years | The calculation handles uneven cash flows.
- It always overstates the payback period | It tends to flatter the project rather than understate it.
- It cannot be used alongside other techniques | The chapter presents the techniques as a set used together.
why: Payback is simple and, where interest and inflation are low, a reasonable forecast. Its blind spot is that a pound in year four is treated as a pound now, which makes the project look better than DCF shows it to be.

stem: A project shows a healthy cumulative cash surplus from year three but a negative net present value at the organisation's discount rate. What does this tell you?
+ The project repays its costs in cash terms but does not clear the organisation's required rate of return | NPV discounts future cash flows, so a positive payback can still be a negative NPV.
- The project is profitable and should proceed | The negative NPV says it fails the required return.
- The discount rate has been applied incorrectly | A negative NPV with a positive payback is a normal result.
- Payback and NPV are the same calculation and one is wrong | They are different techniques measuring different things.
- The project should be appraised using IRR only | IRR is one of several techniques, and NPV is preferred for value.
- The cash flows must be spread evenly to be valid | NPV handles uneven cash flows.
why: Payback asks when the cumulative cash turns positive. NPV asks whether the money, once discounted, beats the return the organisation requires. A project can satisfy the first and fail the second.
